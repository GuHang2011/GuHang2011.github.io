Where do fusion gains come from in low-resource multimodal fake news detection? Operating points, archive artifacts and frozen CLIP baselines

The revised study asks where a score improvement comes from before attributing it to a new model capability.

The revised manuscript has been submitted to Neurocomputing. This status follows my update on 24 October 2026; it does not imply acceptance or a particular review stage.

01

What the revision examines

The manuscript reanalyzes saved predictions from 344 training runs. FCP is the token-level fusion model being examined against simple baselines with shared frozen CLIP features, splits and seeds; it is not presented as a new architecture.

01 / Compare the operating point

Some low-shot GossipCop Macro-F1 advantages track a change in the decision operating point; a higher score alone does not establish a stronger representation.

02 / Audit the data

Archive markers and near-duplicate content can confound evaluation on recollected news datasets.

03 / Account for the full budget

The manuscript limits training labels but uses a fully labeled validation set. The proposed work will count selection and calibration labels too.

02

What the evidence does not establish

  • The study covers two English recollected news datasets, with limited seeds and small test sets. It does not establish a universal conclusion about multimodal fusion.
  • The archived outputs are hard predictions. AUROC, score calibration and post-hoc threshold retuning cannot be recovered from those labels alone.
  • Low-resource training still uses a fully labeled validation set for model selection; this is not an end-to-end few-label budget.
  • KICP is linked as related implementation. This portfolio update neither reruns the benchmark nor declares the repository a complete archive of all revised-paper experiments.
03

How this informs my research plan

For multimodal emotion recognition, I propose counting training, model-selection and calibration labels together; auditing groups and near duplicates; and saving per-example scores and versions. Multiclass transfer, selective prediction and continual updates are proposed studies, not results of this paper.
