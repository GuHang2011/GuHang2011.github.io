RESEARCH DIRECTION / DISCUSSION DRAFT

Reliable multimodal emotion recognition under resource constraints

Learn with fewer labels. Recognize insufficient evidence. Update without losing what already works.

This proposed agenda connects my multimodal-evaluation research with engineering experience in interface states, data workflows and recovery. The methods and outcomes below remain to be tested.

01

Three research questions

RQ01 · Budget-aware adaptation

When is a fusion gain real?

Investigate
Compare simple frozen-feature baselines and lightweight adapters under the same total label budget. Include model selection and calibration, audit near duplicates, and inspect class-wise recall and prediction proportions.
Evidence
Paired seeds, validation resamples, resource cost and artifact-stratified results. A gain must survive the predefined controls.

RQ02 · Evidence-aware prediction

When should a model ask for review?

Investigate
Combine modality quality, missing-input masks and cross-modal disagreement. Compare fixed fusion with quality-aware gating and calibration under noise and missing modalities.
Evidence
Risk–coverage trade-offs, calibration and mechanism ablations. Unknown-class detection and zero-shot recognition use separate protocols.

RQ03 · Controlled continual updates

Can new knowledge be added reliably?

Investigate
Update lightweight modules with a fixed replay budget. Check new and old classes before enabling a candidate; version preprocessing, weights, class descriptions and calibration together.
Evidence
Repeated class orders, forgetting, calibration drift and update cost. Independent test data do not drive version selection.
02

Why this direction

Neurocomputing · Revised submission

My first-author revision reanalyzes 344 archived training runs. It distinguishes operating-point changes and archive artifacts from representation gains. Submitted to Neurocomputing; not accepted. The proposed emotion-recognition work tests whether these evaluation lessons transfer to a new, multiclass task.

From evaluation to implementation

The DASFAA 2027 submission supports validation-sensitivity analysis. KAPT provides KANO-derived learning tools with explicit state and synthetic checks. Frontend and workflow cases support interfaces, version checks and recovery; they are engineering preparation, not emotion-recognition results.

03

A testable research sequence

  1. 01

    Establish baselines

    Use authorized datasets, explicit groups and a full labeling budget; freeze the protocol before testing.

  2. 02

    Test decision mechanisms

    Evaluate corruption, missing evidence and selective prediction with controlled ablations.

  3. 03

    Evaluate updates

    Compare class-incremental updates and build an auditable prototype with consistent rollback.

Predictive scores, labels, grouping and model versions will be logged for each example. New methods must be justified by controlled comparisons; no performance target is presented as an achieved result.
