## 研究问题与方法

结合时序融合 Transformer（TFT）与基于图神经网络的气候风险传播模型（GNN-CRP），研究中国 30 个省份 2005—2024 年可再生能源—水系统的时间风险变化与跨区域级联风险。

- 研究问题：复合极端气候如何影响相互依赖的能源与水系统，并在区域间传播？
- TFT：建模非线性时间依赖。
- GNN-CRP：刻画区域间气候风险传播路径。
- 综合分析：将时间变化与区域关联结合，讨论级联脆弱性、韧性阈值及适应性治理。

## 摘要报告的发现

1. 东西部风险暴露存在差异；水电依赖较高、水资源压力较大的省份系统脆弱性更突出。
2. 相互连接的基础设施可能传递气候冲击，放大运行中断与治理挑战。
3. 绿色基础设施投入、适应性水治理及分布式可再生能源整合，与较低系统风险和较好韧性表现相关。此处不把相关关系写成因果结论。

## English abstract supplied by the author

This research proposes a new hybrid artificial intelligence system to assess multi-hazard climate risk governance in renewable energy-water nexus systems in 30 provinces in China from 2005 to 2024. The Study examines the increasing fragility of energy and water systems to compound climate extremes, such as floods, hydrological variability, droughts, and heat waves, which increasingly jeopardize the sustainable governance of energy and water resources and regional resilience. This work proposes an approach that combines a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) model with a Graph Neural Network-based Climate Risk Propagation (GNN-CRP) framework to tackle the shortcomings of traditional climate-risk models. The integrated TFT–GNN architecture addresses nonlinear temporal dependence and interregional climate-risk transmission pathways in a single framework, offering a dynamic evaluation of cascading climate vulnerabilities between interconnected renewable energy and water systems. The empirical analysis shows substantial spatial differences in climate-risk exposure between China's eastern and western provinces, with provinces that rely more on hydropower and are more water-stressed being the most vulnerable in terms of systemic vulnerability. The findings also show that climate hazards cascade through the interlinked infrastructure network, exacerbating operational disruptions and governance challenges during extreme climate events. The hybrid framework improves risk prediction, identifies risk propagation, and pinpoints resilience thresholds that trigger adaptive policy intervention, outperforming traditional machine learning and econometric approaches. Moreover, investments in green infrastructure, adaptive water governance, and the decentralised integration of renewable energy are associated with lower systemic climate risks and improved regional resilience performance. The Study offers a novel interdisciplinary modeling approach for climate-resilient decision-making and key policy guidance for sustainable infrastructure governance in a climate-uncertain world. Future research should also build on real-time satellite observations, the dynamics of carbon transitions, and cross-border climate-risk spillover effects in adaptive governance systems.
